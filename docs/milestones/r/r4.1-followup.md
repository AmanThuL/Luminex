# R4.1 follow-up — Parent-calibrated parity for the generics candidate

**Status**: Proposed

The owner chose this follow-up on 2026-09-25 after reviewing the [R4.1 validation](r4.1-validation.md).
It re-measures candidate A under a rule that separates two questions R4.1's single hash rule
conflated: does A change what the scene pass computes, and does the parent repeat its own output.
The R4.1 DEFER and its evidence stay as recorded; this follow-up relabels nothing. Candidate B stays
rejected, because its runtime `masked` branch fails R4's boundary whatever its pixels show.

## Why R4.1's rule could not decide A

R4.1 required every hash a candidate produced to also come from the parent in the same session. In
`san-miguel-manual-taa-1` the parent produced 8 distinct hashes in 8 captures and in
`san-miguel-auto-taa-1` 6 in 8, so a build computing exactly what the parent computes would still
fail except by chance. Native TAA does not create this variation. The scene pass's diffuse
derivative path varies between runs (the [M6.5 investigation](../m6/m6.5.md#open-parity-investigation)
localized that path), and history carries a difference from any of the 32 frames into the captured
image. San Miguel with TAA off repeated in all 8 runs.

This was foreseeable from the project's records before R4.1 measured: `reference.json`'s evidence
limit, M6.5's open investigation and the TAA repeat counts in R3.x parity runs all show it. The R4.1
design ran its coverage smoke once and never measured the parent's repeatability per case. This
follow-up adds that measurement before any candidate capture.

## Builds and branch

SHA-256 `171f6fe9…`. Before any capture: each worktree is clean at its tagged commit; each App
binary matches the hash R4.1's session header recorded; and `static_compare.py` is rerun for A and B,
whose reproduction check proves each build's `.metal` equals what its tagged source compiles to. The
hashes of every `Shaders/*.metal` and `*.metallib` are then recorded and checked again after stage 2.
Any mismatch stops the follow-up. No source or build changes.

which applies the rules below and calls `Tools/Screenshots/compare.py`'s `image_difference` with

## Stage 1 — Parent repeatability

H alone renders all 29 R4.1 cases for 8 rounds in fresh processes, with the R4.1 command,
environment and coverage log. A case is **repeatable** when its 8 captures share one hash, and
**non-repeatable** otherwise. The classification is frozen before stage 2 starts and is not revised
afterwards. Stage 1 images are classification evidence only; they enter no stage 2 comparison.

## Stage 2 — Fresh three-build session

A new R4.1-shaped session: 29 cases, 8 rounds, builds H, A and B, order rotating by round, all rounds
run, the cap not extended. R4.1's images train and score nothing. B runs as a positive control.

**Repeatable cases** use R4.1's strict rule unchanged: a case is unchanged for a candidate when
every hash it produced also came from H in the session.

**Non-repeatable cases** use a leave-one-round-out comparison under the `strict` profile, which
allows at most 1% differing pixels and at most 0.05% of pixels differing by more than 8 of 255:

- *Null control.* For each round r, H's image is compared with each H image from the other seven
  rounds; it passes when at least one of those seven comparisons passes `strict`. The case's null
  control holds when all 8 H images pass.
- *Candidate.* For each round r, the candidate's image is compared with each H image from the other
  seven rounds, the same pool size as the null control; it passes when at least one of those seven
  comparisons passes `strict`. The candidate passes the case when all 8 of its images pass.
- *Power.* B's result in the same comparison is reported per case. The rule has shown power when B
  fails at least one non-repeatable case; a non-repeatable case B passes is listed as not
  discriminating.

For each image the nearest H image, the one with the fewest differing pixels, is reported with its
differing pixels, pixels over 8 and maximum channel delta, along with whether the image's hash
occurred from H. These are for the record; none of them adds a gate.

## Decision rule

A passes the follow-up when all of these hold:

1. the build checks above hold before stage 1 and after stage 2, so R4.1's static and suite gates
   still describe the measured builds;
2. stage 2 is complete, every H capture carries its coverage line, and all eight variant and entry
   cells are covered;
3. every repeatable case is unchanged for A under the strict hash rule;
4. every non-repeatable case holds its null control and A passes it;
5. the rule shows power in at least one non-repeatable case.

If a non-repeatable case fails its null control, the parent does not meet the `strict` profile
against itself there; the case is **unmeasurable**, the follow-up is incomplete and R4.1's DEFER
stands. If stage 1 finds no non-repeatable case, items 4 and 5 are vacuous and the strict hash rule
alone decides. A failure is recorded, not retried until passing.

Passing is a recommendation, not an adoption: R4.2 still needs the owner's adopt decision, the
owner's confirmation of the recorded inference that A's MSL differences, none arithmetic, do not
block adoption, and a choice of whether R4.2's gate carries this two-tier rule and the 14 extension
cases.

## Alternatives rejected

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| The `repeatability.py` min/max envelope | In M7.2 fresh parent controls escaped an eight-sample envelope, so it would fail its own parent here |
| More rounds under the hash rule | With 8 distinct parent hashes in 8 runs the rule does not converge at any fixed cap |
| Dropping the San Miguel TAA cases | It changes the matrix after results and leaves masked motion parity on VisibilityLab alone |
| Thresholds from R4.1's pixel diagnostic | They were chosen after seeing A's numbers; `strict` predates R4 |

## Boundaries

No shader, host, binding, uniform, label or build change. No threshold other than the existing
`strict` profile; no case added or dropped after stage 1 starts. The R4.1 record, its DEFER and its
evidence are not edited except to link this follow-up. The follow-up does not investigate the cause
of San Miguel TAA non-repeatability; that remains the M6.5 open investigation.
