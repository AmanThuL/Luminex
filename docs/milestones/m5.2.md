# M5.2 — RHI Frame-Data Path and Public Surface

**Status**: Implemented (2026-08-12)

M5.2 shipped ADR 0010's bounded reshape into production. `CommandList::setUniforms` is removed
with no compatibility alias; `bindFrameData(slot, value)` replaces it as one typed operation that
allocates, copies the caller's block, binds its GPU address into the argument table, and returns
that `GpuAddress` — a standard-layout value with no arithmetic. Per-frame data now lives in
retirement-safe, growable per-slot page arenas (256 KiB normal pages; a request too large for the
active page gets a new page rounded up to that quantum, never a per-draw GPU object) instead of a
fixed-capacity ring; `bindBuffer` is unchanged and remains the path for static or caller-owned
data that survives the frame. The monolithic public `RHI.h` split into self-contained per-concept
leaf headers (`GpuAddress.h`, `Format.h`, `Buffer.h`, `Texture.h`, `Heap.h`, `Sampler.h`,
`ShaderLibrary.h`, `GraphicsPipeline.h`, `ComputePipeline.h`, `Indirect.h`, `RenderPass.h`,
`CommandList.h`, `Swapchain.h`, `Device.h`, plus the existing `Result.h`, `Validate.h`, and
`CaptureSchema.h`) behind an includes-only `RHI.h` umbrella. The M5.1 NoApi evidence was not used
as a maintained library; its final source is archived at tag `m5.1-noapi-evidence`.

## Evidence

- **Checkpoint-A interpretation (controller ruling).** ADR 0009's freeze binds case identities,
  tags, assertions, and tolerances — not the delivery call inside a frozen case's setup. All 39
  `setUniforms` uses across the 7 checkpoint-A-bearing files migrated their delivery call only;
  10 of those cases carry `[checkpoint-a]` and were verified delivery-call-only (13 lines changed,
  every one a single delivery-call pair, names/tags/assertions/tolerances byte-identical). The case
  "uniform ring survives twelve frames overlapping in flight" retains its historical name despite
  no longer describing a ring. One arena case (`GpuFrameDataTests`' twelve-frame case) was
  additively tagged `[checkpoint-a]`, which ADR 0009 permits without superseding the freeze, so the
  frozen filter grew from 18 cases / 1,749 assertions (M5.1) to **19 cases / 1,856 assertions**,
  confirmed unchanged at both the pre-fix and post-fix collection tips.
- **Migration correctness.** Screenshots are byte-identical across all three scenes (Sponza,
  Damaged Helmet, MaterialLab) with bloom on and off, comparing pre-migration and migrated builds.
  `F-FIT-512`'s readback digest is identical across the frozen baseline tag and every candidate tip
  measured (`d3afc993d6aaffa5` at the neutralization control, unchanged through the write-combined
  fix). Every workload's baseline/candidate digest matched on all 12 repetitions of both the
  pre-fix and post-fix scored collections — zero mismatches.
- **Paired performance results — accepted evidence (post-fix collection).** 12 paired AB/BA
  repetitions per workload, 16 warm-up + 256 measured frames per side, release builds, Metal
  validation and capture off, one MacBook Pro (Mac15,9, Apple M3 Max, 12P+4E, 64 GB RAM), macOS
  26.5.2 (25F84), Xcode 26.6 (17F113), AC power at 80%, no thermal/performance warning recorded.
  Medians are the median of each side's 12 per-repetition medians; CIs are two-sided 95% paired
  bootstrap over percentage deltas (M5.1's fixed-resample method). The Pre-fix column is the first
  scored collection, retained for the record (see the regression story below); the accepted
  evidence is the Post-fix column and its CI.

  |---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
  | `F-DYNAMIC-1024` | materialWin ≥25%, CI excl. 0 | +90.06% | +94.56% | [94.47, 94.60] | **PASS** |
  | `F-DYNAMIC-4096` | materialWin ≥25%, CI excl. 0 | +96.61% | +97.70% | [97.67, 97.71] | **PASS** |

  | `F-STATIC-4096` | withinRegressionBound, lower ≥ −5% | +0.59% | −0.78% | [−1.15, −0.32] | **PASS** |

  5 of 5 workloads pass post-fix; every acceptance rule in spec section 11 holds. No rerun of the
  post-fix scored collection was needed — the driver reported 5/5 comparable, 12/12 pairs each, on
  the first frozen run.
- **Counter evidence.** Across all 60 post-fix candidate raw JSONs (12 reps × 5 workloads, and
  identically across the 60 pre-fix runs): zero arena-page creations during any measured frame
  after the three slots reached their high water (`pageCreationsAfterWarmup ==
  pageCreationsAfterMeasurement` on every run); `addressBinds == calls` and `bytes == calls ×
  blockSize` with zero exceptions; both static cases report `calls == 0` and `bytes == 0` on all 12
  repetitions each. Per-draw GPU buffer creation is zero by construction, not just by counter — the
  candidate delivery path is exactly one `bindFrameData` call per draw, and static buffers are
  built once before the timed loop.
- **Baseline-composition disclosure (controller ruling).** The incumbent baseline's dynamic-case
  cost is not purely an allocation-path tax. Fitting `cost(n) = a·n + b·n²` through the two dynamic
  cases' measured **pre-fix** baseline medians (`a` = 2,873.39 ns, `b` = 0.7542 ns per
  already-registered resource — derived from the pre-fix collection's baseline side, which the fix
  did not touch) attributes `CaptureSchema::upsert`'s per-`createBuffer` linear registry-scan term
  — present whether or not a capture is active, since it is registration bookkeeping, not capture
  recording — to **11.85%** of `F-DYNAMIC-1024`'s baseline median (512 overflow buffers/frame) and
  **44.64%** of `F-DYNAMIC-4096`'s (3,072 overflow buffers/frame). The headline win is therefore not
  purely "removed an O(n²) tax": the candidate also removes the *linear* per-overflow-buffer
  creation cost any buffer-per-block strategy would pay regardless of scan complexity, which is a
  majority of `F-DYNAMIC-1024`'s baseline cost. The capture-off `O(n)` `createBuffer` registry scan
  is recorded here as an **incumbent finding**, out of this milestone's scope to fix, alongside
  M5.1's recorded incumbent findings. The frozen two-point method (only the two dynamic cases'
  data points) biases the reported O(n²) share **low**: a three-point check that also subtracts a
  per-draw encode floor derived from `F-FIT-512`'s own baseline moves the shares from 11.85%→12.30%
  (`F-DYNAMIC-1024`) and 44.64%→46.35% (`F-DYNAMIC-4096`) — a small correction in the same
  direction, so the conclusion (the registry-scan term is a real and growing share, not the whole
  story) is robust to this simplification rather than an artifact of it.
- **Timed-region note.** The measured region wraps `beginFrame` (pacing wait, retired-timing
  publish, allocator reset) on both baseline and candidate sides, matching the M5.1 precedent, and
  deviates from a literal reading of the spec's "starts after the pacing wait"; the harness's
  per-frame `waitIdle` makes the wait itself a no-op, so the deviation does not change what is
  measured.
- **The `F-FIT-512` regression and its resolution.** The first scored collection
  measured `bindFrameData` at **−32.20%** [−32.43, −31.28] on the fitting workload — every one of
  12 repetitions regressed, and a same-day rerun under checked machine conditions reproduced it
  almost exactly (−32.25% [−32.83, −31.60]), ruling out a one-off artifact. The investigation's
  cross-TU-call hypothesis (non-inlined `validateFrameData` and `Metal4FrameArena::allocate` calls,
  a capture-recording check, three tally increments) was tested by neutralization, not accepted:
  removing each suspect in turn from scratch builds measured a combined **≈1.7 ns/call**, under 5%
  of the 35.9 ns/call gap to close. The hypothesis is disproved. The actual cause is the frame-data
  pages' CPU cache mode: `MTLCPUCacheModeWriteCombined` maps the range non-cacheable on Apple
  silicon, and `bindFrameData` writes small (64 B) blocks scattered across a caller-chosen stride
  rather than one streaming run, so nothing coalesces — measured at **42.6 ns/call** of uncached
  scattered stores (M3 Max). The fix that dropped the write-combined hint allocates pages under the
  default (non-write-combined) shared storage mode and inlines the arena's common-case allocate
  path into the header; growth semantics, assertions, and diagnostics are otherwise unchanged. The
  42.6 ns/call figure, fitted on `F-FIT-512` alone, was confirmed out-of-sample on `F-DYNAMIC-4096`
  (same 64-byte block, 4,096 calls/frame instead of 512): predicted **−174.3 µs/frame**, measured
  **−182.3 µs/frame**, a 4% miss at 8× the call count. Both collections' headline numbers are
  published in the Pre-fix/Post-fix columns of the results table above; the **post-fix set is the
  accepted evidence**. The spec's owner corrected section 5's backend-mapping text to describe
  shared storage under the default CPU cache mode rather than write-combined.
- **`F-STATIC-4096` drift resolution.** The pre-fix collection showed a within-run monotone ramp
  (median of the first 32 measured frames versus the last 32) on both sides — 1 of 12 baseline runs
  over 1.2× (1.279×) against 5 of 12 candidate runs (1.361×–1.746×) — that clusters by time window
  independent of AB/BA order (occurrences track the clock, not the alternation), which is why it
  still passed the median-bootstrap gate without the ramp being resolved. The post-fix collection
  reduced it to 0 baseline / 1 candidate run over 1.2×, and running the case first and alone in a
  fresh session eliminated it entirely (0/12 both sides, worst ratio 1.169, verdict unchanged:
  median −0.6158%, CI [−0.9055, +0.0050]). Thermal/DVFS accumulation from the two heavy dynamic
  workloads that precede this case in the frozen protocol is the leading explanation, **stated here
  as inference, not measurement** — the decoupled rerun confirms the effect is order/time-related,
  not what causes it. The small post-fix sign flip (+0.59% pre-fix to −0.78% post-fix) cannot be a
  `bindFrameData` cost, since the counters prove this workload makes zero `bindFrameData` calls; it
  is attributed to object-code layout from the inlined `allocate` reshaping
  `Metal4CommandList.o`, is well inside the −5% bound, and the three independent readings straddle
  zero.

## Deliberate limits

- **Oversized pages reserve alignment padding.** A grown page is sized from
  `size + alignment - 1`, then rounded to the 256 KiB quantum. Checked arithmetic rejects an
  unrepresentable public request before allocation, so every accepted alignment fits regardless of
  the GPU base Metal returns.
- **`GpuAddress` carries no arithmetic.** It is a standard-layout, trivially copyable value with
  equality and an explicit validity test; general address arithmetic was not added because no
  caller demonstrated the need.
- **The frame-data counters are test and capture evidence, not a public performance API.** They are
  exposed through the Metal 4 extension header under `RHI/Include/RHI/Metal4/` for tests and
  captures, not through the backend-neutral core headers.
- **Single machine, one scale.** All measurements are from one M3 Max under the frozen paired
  protocol; the environment and every caveat are recorded above and in the task evidence, mirroring
  M5.1's scope honesty.
- **`F-STATIC-4096`'s noise is real and only partly explained.** Its within-run drift is a
  time-window effect that is not candidate-specific (the pre-fix baseline shows it too) and mostly
  resolves when the case is decoupled from the workloads that precede it in the frozen protocol,
  but the specific mechanism (thermal versus DVFS versus something else) is inference, not measured
  root cause, and the residual post-fix sign flip on a zero-call workload is attributed to
  object-code layout rather than proven with a profiler.
- **Two pre-existing `gputrace_dump.py` gaps surfaced during capture verification, neither caused
  by this migration:** a blob-join heuristic limitation where the three frame-data pages (confirmed
  by the schema sidecar to be exactly one page per slot, no growth) fail the tool's normal
  label-join path, and a stale `lmx.render.sceneColor` entry in the tool's hardcoded
  expected-resource checklist (the renderer has used `lmx.render.sceneColorHdr` since before this
  migration). Both are left unfixed as out of this milestone's scope.
