## Permanent Sponza lights and individual enable state

Sponza now authors 16 static local lights before scene finalization: eight points and eight spots.
They appear in the Local lights Hierarchy immediately. Each row has an enable checkbox scoped by
the full `LightId`; Rendering > Lighting no longer contains a Sponza rig toggle.

`LocalLight::enabled` defaults true. Disabling retains the generational ID, row slot, colour,
intensity, range, cone, position and animation binding. The same validated light produces an inert
GPU row with zero range/bound radius, so Direct, Clustered and the reach diagnostic ignore it.
Disabled lights still occupy the 4096-identity capacity and remain selectable/editable. An orbit
continues sampling while disabled; Stop restores only the captured animation-owned position and
preserves enablement and other edits. Per-light Reset restores the authored enablement and fields,
with an orbit-owned position sampled at the current time.

`Scene::localLights()` and Scene tables identity counts include disabled lights;
`Scene::enabledLightCount()` and rendering `liveLightCount` count enabled lights. With all lights
disabled, every requested local-light mode is effective Off, imports no light table and declares
no cluster/debug pass. The Lighting reading is labelled **Enabled lights**. Rendering's scoped
Lighting Reset restores mode/diagnostics and clears the LightLab pile; it does not disable Sponza
lights or undo individual light edits.

Sponza-only `--local-light-rig on|off` remains as a deterministic regression/content control,
with **on by default**. Explicit off disables the authored group without deleting its IDs or
allocated rows. Thus it supplies zero enabled lights, not the historical zero-allocation scene.
`--local-lights off` alone disables contribution while leaving enabled population nonzero; it is
not a zero-live fixture. Screenshot, sequence, measurement and editor startup apply the explicit
rig setting. Other scenes retain their own authored lighting and reject this Sponza-only flag.

The editor disables Hierarchy/Inspector edits during an active Measure run. Model validation also
fixes enabled population at the first submitted declaration, including warmup, and rejects a later
population change. Starting a measurement with some or all lights disabled is valid; no report
schema change is needed.

## Sponza two-level camera tour

`authorSponzaCameraTour` supplies a deterministic, closed **120-second** rail through both corridor
levels. The lower camera height is 1.6 m and the upper height is 5.8 m, above floors near 0 and
4.15 m in the pinned metre-scale asset. The camera crosses the open atrium between levels; the
asset has no connecting staircase, so this is a flythrough rather than a walking route.

Rounded cubic turns, arc-length pacing and symmetric look-ahead smooth speed/orientation at
corridor joins. Yaw is unwrapped before key interpolation. The route is baked at the existing
animation rate, loops back to its first pose, sets the initial camera to that pose and preserves
the lens and other animation tracks. The editor still starts Stopped; Play with Follow camera
rail follows the tour. LightLab's separate 12-second rail is unchanged.

The route analysis covers 7201 sampled camera-centre positions against 262267 asset triangles.
Its minimum sampled centre-to-triangle clearance is **0.269832 m**; route length is approximately
166.51 m and speed 1.388 m/s. Scripts and the map are retained under `geometry-review/`. This checks
sampled centres, not the full view frustum, a camera collision volume or every continuous point
between samples; it is not a complete collision proof.

Actual editor Play was observed at 30.8 s in the lower corridor and 109.25 s during the atrium
descent. `editor/04-tour-running.png`, `05-lower-corridor-30s.png` and

## LightLab flicker: two distinct corrections

### Emitter/surface crossing

The localized orange pop was traced to light 154 entering a pillar: its orbit crossed the pillar's
x = 5.65 m face around t = 10.55 s. A punctual emitter inside geometry can move from the surface's
back hemisphere directly into the steep inverse-square near field. This is an authored-content
singularity; a cluster assignment discrepancy is not needed to create the change.

The authored grid now clears the material rows by **0.25 m over each entire orbit**. The shared
light/track generator expands the pillar/sphere row bounds by orbit radius plus clearance and
moves affected orbit centres outside those bounds. Static lights use radius zero. Range law,
intensity, height, orbit radius/speed and the attenuation equation remain unchanged. This corrects
where emitters travel; it does not add local-light shadows, occlusion or a new attenuation clamp.

### Undersampled punctual specular highlights

A separate residual shimmer on low-roughness surfaces is addressed in the shared punctual-light
accumulator with the conservative isotropic normal-footprint filter attributed to equation 13 of
[Tokuyoshi and Kaplanyan (2021)](https://jcgt.org/published/0010/02/02/).
The implementation computes normal screen derivatives before the per-froxel divergent loop:

```text
variance = 1 / (2 pi)
kernel = min(2 * variance * (dot(ddx(N), ddx(N)) + dot(ddy(N), ddy(N))), 0.18)
filteredAlpha = sqrt(saturate(alpha * alpha + kernel))
```

Direct and Clustered use this same filtered alpha. Authored roughness, directional lighting and
IBL are unchanged; the filter broadens only punctual specular lobes. It is a spatial anti-aliasing
change, not extra temporal accumulation, a global roughness increase or a history-reset workaround.
It can change local-light brightness/appearance; exact historical lit pixels are not expected.

### Recorded diagnostic metrics and limits

`filter-quality-analysis.json` compares clearance-only and clearance-plus-filter captures of
LightLab 256, pile 0, at 1280×720 and scale 1. Each sequence contains 64 frames after warmup 600
(simulation frames 600–663, t = 10–11.05 s), with the authored moving camera, manual EV0 and bloom
on. Temporal Off and Native TAA are separate comparisons. `filter-quality-method.md` defines the
ROI and metrics: project the sphere centred at (-1.25, 0.9, -3.5) m, radius 0.9 m, from the authored
rail; bilinearly affine-resample its projected square to 128×128; evaluate the interior circle of
radius 57.6 pixels about (63.5, 63.5). This aligns position/scale, not true surface motion.

Final PNG sRGB is decoded to display-linear RGB and weighted (0.2126, 0.7152, 0.0722). The aligned
metric is RMS of `L[t+2] - 2L[t+1] + L[t]` over time and interior pixels. The historical JSON key
`integrated_energy_second_difference_rms` applies that same operator to each frame's mean displayed
luminance: it is **not scene-linear radiometric energy**. Mean luminance averages the same domain.

| Reconstruction / variant | Aligned luminance second-difference RMS | Mean-luminance second-difference RMS | Mean luminance |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Off / clearance only | 0.13245040 | 0.06104652 | 0.49602500 |
| Off / plus filter | 0.02531011 | 0.00813013 | 0.51162734 |
| TAA / clearance only | 0.00984654 | 0.00192757 | 0.33361558 |
| TAA / plus filter | 0.00767757 | 0.00197877 | 0.43929031 |

The aligned second-difference RMS falls 80.89% with temporal Off and 22.03% with TAA. Mean-luminance
second-difference RMS falls 86.68% with Off but **rises 2.66% with TAA**. Mean luminance
rises 3.15% and 31.68%, respectively. Keep those brightness changes and the mixed TAA metric
visible; a lower temporal variation metric alone does not establish better fidelity or radiometric
energy preservation. Moving highlights, approximate alignment, bloom/tone mapping and reconstruction
all affect these measurements. There is no ground-truth image, uncertainty interval, new acceptance threshold
or full-scene/all-density quality claim here.

All 64 recorded filtered Direct/Clustered temporal-Off frame pairs have zero differing pixels.
This is a scoped consistency observation for that sequence, not a replacement for the full F2/F3
correctness inventory. In the actual editor, LightLab was played and paused at 2.65 s and 1.05 s;
`editor/08-lightlab-filtered-paused.png` and `09-lightlab-looped-paused.png` retain those states.
The exact t = 10–11.05 s diagnostic interval was captured only by the 64-frame offscreen sequences,
not manually replayed at those same times in the editor. Those scopes must not be conflated or
reported as a full-loop visual proof. The new full-rail list oracle results are recorded below.

## F5 diagnosis and exact zero-enabled causal gate

The original exact same-binary mode comparison passed **11/15 cells and failed overall**, with
seven failed comparisons in four Sponza temporal cells. Their Off/Off nulls also differed. The
parent comparison remains strict 12/15 and established scoped vendor-profile 15/15. These are

The separately named **exact zero-enabled causal gate** is adopted for this follow-up; its final
integration result is **PASS**. It asks whether requesting Off, Direct or Clustered changes the
GPU computation or temporal continuity when enabled population is zero. Independent replay also
asks whether separate executions reproduce every source sample and temporal input/state. An
identical-Off control that already differs cannot isolate a local-light-mode effect. This is a
change in the question tested, not a tolerance adjustment or a retrospective pass for original F5.

### Controlled diagnosis

Same-process Sponza source-HDR differences occur before reconstruction. The fetched asset is
opaque; curtain/column localization does not imply masked-material borders. GPU scene-table and
depth bytes match. Separate exact-bit probes find interpolated UV.x and UV.y unchanged, while
`du/dx` differs at 16282 pixels and computed LOD at 12350 pixels on the first Native TAA 1.0 frame.
Fixed base-colour LOD 0 and 1 each pass their 32-frame cold Native controls; explicit
`SampleGrad(ddx(UV), ddy(UV))` does not. These experiments isolate a derivative-dependent sampling
path, **not its underlying mechanism**, and do not establish TAA or vendor scaling as the cause.

A single persistent Renderer supplies a further ordering control. With temporal Off, the first
round's subsequent HDR differing-byte counts are `[0, 81271, 81271, 81271]` for both request orders
`[Off, Direct, Clustered, Off]` and `[Clustered, Direct, Off, Off]`; rounds 2–4 have no HDR differences.
The unchanged pattern under reordering follows execution position rather than the named mode in
this control. It neither explains every original F5 cell nor resolves Sponza replay repeatability.
The valid `single-renderer-*-attempt2` and `single-renderer-permuted-attempt1` evidence is retained;
the earlier reset-harness failure remains identified in `single-renderer-cut-attempt1-HARNESS-INVALID.md`.

The [Metal language specification](https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf)
defines perspective interpolation (§5.4), fragment derivatives (§6.11.1) and derivative-based
implicit LOD (§6.13). Those definitions do not identify the cause of these observations or justify
calling them expected hardware behavior. No fixed-LOD `SampleLevel`, reduced texture quality,
reconstruction fallback or sampling workaround is adopted from these diagnostics.

A separate source review found an editor transition issue: requested-mode changes asked for a
camera cut even when zero enabled lights kept the effective mode Off. The correction suppresses
that mode-only cut, including Reset and implicit diagnostic-mode selection, while retaining
content/live-mode resets. This is not the established cause of separate-process CLI F5 differences.

### Exact causal contract and current result

1. Require exact semantic graph, pass/resource/barrier, pipeline, binding and shader-consumed
   uniform invariants across requests and reconstruction cells. Include never-populated,
   removed-last-light, disabled-last-light and previously populated Clustered slot states. No
   local-light import, cluster/debug pass or timed list work may appear with zero enabled lights.
2. Require a real-GPU production-shader fixture with nontrivial opaque/masked/sky content,
   repeated mode changes and all three paced slots. Compare source HDR/depth/motion/reactive,
   exposure, applicable owned history and final output exactly; reject nonfinite HDR.
3. Compare temporal outputs only with equal current inputs **and equivalent incoming history,
   exposure and reset state**. Equal MRTs after an earlier divergent frame do not establish equal
   history. Report qualifying comparisons; an empty qualifying set cannot pass the causal gate.
4. Require matching camera-cut/reset schedules, jitter progression and requested/effective
   reconstruction, with no mode-induced extra reset or fallback. Vendor private history is not
   directly byte-compared or claimed cloned merely because reset was requested; check exposed
   adapter inputs/state and actual output within the controlled fixture's scope.
5. Retain independent Sponza replay as a failed/unresolved diagnostic, alongside the original
   nulls, parent comparisons, F3 temporal metrics and visual inspection. Do not subtract null
   differences, derive a tolerance from failed pixels or apply a blanket vendor exception.

The final production fixture includes opaque geometry, an actual checker alpha mask, sky, and a
retained disabled light identity. Six cells × 32 frames × five synchronized histories at 160×90
produce **7040 exact attachment/table comparisons, zero differences** (`final-causal-fixture.tsv`).
Cells are Off, jittered Raw 1.0, Native TAA 1.0/0.5 and MetalFX 1.0/0.5; histories are Off
baseline/repeat, constant Direct, constant Clustered and cycling requests. HDR is finite and
nonflat; source MRTs/exposure remain equal from the common first frame, so all owned-history
comparisons qualify. Vendor private history remains outside the direct-readback claim.

A separate regression first populates all three paced slots with Clustered lights and verifies
that their HDR differs from Off. It then disables the last identity and checks every mode × slot
combination against the zero-light HDR, while rejecting any light import/pass. Never-populated and
removed-last-light checks remain. CPU graph/used-input tests also cover opaque, masked and sky
pipeline declarations and temporal/reset state. This completes the exact causal gate without a
pixel tolerance, discarded warmup, fixed LOD or reconstruction fallback.
